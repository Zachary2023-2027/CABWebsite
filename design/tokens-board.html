Accent and semantic

One accent, a steel marking blue. It reads as ink on a drawing rather than a brand colour, and it is the only hue that is not a warm neutral. Amber, red and green are reserved for warning, error and success, which is why the accent is blue: nothing else was left that does not collide with a state.

Drawing tokens

07

Decisions and open questions

  • Accent is a steel blue. Amber, red and green are committed to warning, error and success. An orange or green accent would collide with a state colour at a glance, which is exactly when it matters. Blue is what was left that still reads as instrument marking. Tell me if you want it warmer and I will retune the whole ramp.
  • Five sizes, plus one exception. The workshop view needs numbers readable at arm's length, which no size in a five step interface scale can do. It is a separate token, fs-workshop at 64px, used on that screen only.
  • Disabled text is below 4.5:1. Deliberate, and the only token that is. Disabled controls are exempt under WCAG, and holding disabled text at 4.5:1 makes it look enabled.
  • Monospace is JetBrains Mono. Guessed. It has a clear 0 with a dot, unambiguous 1 against l, and open apertures at small sizes. IBM Plex Mono is the obvious alternative if you want something drier.
  • Surface is lighter than background in light mode and lighter in dark mode too. Panels lift off the page in both. The alternative, sinking panels, made the hairline borders do too much work.
  • Drawing colours are my invention. There is nothing in the brief that fixes them. They are tuned so a full elevation reads as a drawing rather than a chart, and so every part type is separable in greyscale for printing.
