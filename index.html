From a bare wall to a stack of parts

01

Start with the room

Straight, L or U. Type the wall lengths, mark the window, the meter box or the pipe that is already there, and set the heights you build to. Australian defaults are in already: 900 bench over a 150 kick, 560 base, 320 wall.

02

Fill it with cabinets

Pick from 21 families and they flow along the run in order. Drag one and it pins to the millimetre, snapping to its neighbours, the wall ends and a blind corner. The elevation redraws to scale as you go and the 3D view stands the whole run up beside it.

03

Nest it onto real sheets

Every part is packed onto the sheets you actually stock, with blade width and edge trim taken off, in a guillotine-friendly order a table saw can follow. Waste, keepable offcuts and the cutting sequence come with each sheet.

04

Take it to the bench

Hole positions on the 32mm system, panel by panel, drawn flat as the board sits in front of you. Then a workshop view built for a phone in a shed: three numbers, big, one part at a time, tick and swipe.

Eleven screens, one project

Everything the build needs, in one place.

Planner

The main screen. A searchable cabinet picker, a to-scale elevation and a live 3D room side by side, and an inspector for every number on the cabinet you have selected: width, height, depth, doors, drawers, shelves, drawer heights, blind corner, board species and thickness for that one cabinet alone.

  • Snapping drags and pinned positions
  • Warnings drawn on the cabinet: overlaps, shelf spans, doors too wide, gaps
  • Walk the room in first person at eye height

Cabinet

One cabinet in 3D. Explode it, open or remove the doors, cut a section on any axis and read the part table beside it.

Cut list

Every part in the project, grouped by cabinet or material, filtered how you like. Tick parts off as you cut them. Export CSV.

Nesting

A to-scale layout per sheet with waste, offcuts and a cutting sequence. Set your kerf, trim and smallest useful offcut once and every screen agrees.

Drilling

Shelf pins at 32mm pitch, hinge cups at 35mm, construction holes, setbacks and depths, drawn for every panel that has any.

Hardware

Hinges, runner pairs, handles and bin runners totalled with the cabinets they belong to, plus room for the fittings only you know about.

Workshop

Full screen, one part at a time, for a phone on the bench. The three numbers you are about to set on the saw, and nothing else.

Costing

Board as actually nested, hardware, benchtop and kick, split per cabinet and per linear metre. Put a quote beside it and see the difference in dollars and percent.

Order list

What to actually buy: sheets, fittings, edge tape, benchtop and kickboard, as plain text you can copy into a message.

Print pack

Elevations, cut list, sheet layouts, drilling schedule and shopping list, on A4 or Letter, black on white, paginated so the preview matches the paper.

Checks & Reference

A pass over the whole project before you cut, and the workshop numbers worth having on hand: spans, sheet sizes, runner lengths, appliance cavities.

Where it earns its keep

The optimiser looks for the kitchen
that comes off fewer sheets.

Keep your cabinet types and your count, hold the ones you have locked, and let it search the widths that still fit the wall. Every candidate is nested and costed for real, not guessed at. Same for materials: plans that consolidate the whole kitchen onto one or two boards, and build plans that swap full backs for rails or round your thicknesses to stock. Nothing changes until you press Apply.

Everything in millimetres
One unit, everywhere, all the way to the hole depth. No fractions, no conversions, nothing to get wrong at the saw.
Prices in AUD
Seeded and fully editable: sheets, hinges, runners, handles, bin runners, bench and kick per metre. Labelled as estimates wherever they appear.
Cuts you can follow
The nest is guillotine friendly on purpose. Every cut runs the full width of the piece, because a perfect nest needing plunge cuts is no use in a shed.
Nothing dropped silently
A part too big for any sheet you stock is reported with the size it would need, never quietly binned and never charged for.

No account. No server. No one watching.

The whole thing runs in the browser on your machine. Your kitchen autosaves locally as you work, and a project file is a plain .kcb.json you keep wherever you keep your files. Share a design as a link and the design travels in the link itself.
